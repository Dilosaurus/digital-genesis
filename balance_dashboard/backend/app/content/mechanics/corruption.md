## corruption

Corruption is not damage. Corruption is not a debuff. Corruption is the **language the angels write in**, and every time a pirate plays an angel-touched card, some of that language lodges in their deck and starts whispering.

## how you get it

- **Playing certain cards** — some stolen angelic cards generate corruption when played. The Cryptomancer's whole kit is built around this.
- **Taking hits from corruption-aligned enemies** — angels of the Rift and the Void Core deal corruption damage that bypasses block.
- **Accepting pacts** — when an angel offers a deal mid-run, the price is usually corruption. Accept enough pacts and your deck is more theirs than yours.
- **Standing too long in a corrupted room** — certain rooms *leak*. The longer you stay, the more corruption seeps in.

## what it does

Corruption has two layers.

### The meter

Each operator has a **corruption meter** (0 to 100). It goes up. It rarely goes down. At certain thresholds it applies passive effects:

- **0–24** — normal play. No effects.
- **25+** — the Cryptomancer's passive begins scaling. For everyone else, some enemies start attacking corrupted operators preferentially.
- **50+** — the Cryptomancer reaches full power. For everyone else, cards begin to *mutate*: a `Strike` might randomly transform into a `Corrupt Strike` with different numbers. Not always worse. Often different.
- **75+** — the deep game. Corrupted operators draw extra cards each turn but lose HP each turn. Every run becomes a race against the meter.
- **100** — the operator is *Corrupted*. Their deck is rewritten. Their portrait changes. They can still play, but they are now an expression of the angels' language, not their own.

### The mutation

Every card in an operator's deck has a *corruption state*. When a card becomes corrupted, it transforms into a variant card — usually more powerful, but with a drawback tied to the angels' aesthetics. A `Strike` becomes a `Judgement`. A `Defend` becomes an `Absolution`. A `Backdoor` becomes a `Confession`.

Corrupted cards are **not the same cards**. They have different effects, different costs, different flavour text. Some are build-defining. Some are traps.

The jeweller in the Outer Nexus can remove corruption from cards for a price. The jeweller in the Rift will *trade* you corrupted variants for clean ones. The jeweller in the Void Core does not exist — the Void Core does not permit cleansing.

## how to read it

The lab has a corruption tab at `/lab/tables` that lists every card's corrupted variants side-by-side. Read it before committing to a corruption-heavy build. Some transformations are excellent; some are terrible; and some only make sense when chained with a specific gem.

## what corruption is really

Here is the part nobody writes down in the training materials:

> Corruption is not a mechanic. Corruption is the slow **theological education** of the pirate who is descending into the Nexus. Every point of corruption is a sentence of scripture that has lodged in the pirate's mind. A fully corrupted operator has, effectively, *memorized the angels' holy book*, and they play as if they *believe it*.
>
> Some operators, upon being fully corrupted, refuse to respawn. They stay. They join the angels. GENESIS considers this the worst possible outcome — worse than death, because the operator walked there on their own.
>
> **Watch your meter. Watch your crew's meters. Bring them back before they can read the language fluently.**
