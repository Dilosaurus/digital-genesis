## pacts & tithes

Sometimes, mid-run, an angel will **offer you a pact**. This is not a trick. The angels are not lying to you. The pact is real. The benefits are real. The price is also real, and the price is always structured so that it hurts later, not now.

## how pacts work

When a pact is offered, combat pauses. Every operator in the crew sees the pact text. The crew has **ten seconds** to vote. Majority wins. If the vote is tied, the pact is *accepted by default* — the angels are generous with their defaults.

A pact has two halves:

### the offering

Something good. Usually one of:

- A stat boost for the rest of the run (+1 draw, +1 energy, +20% damage).
- A relic materialized in the crew's inventory.
- A card added to your deck that has powerful effects and no sin tag (yet).
- Full HP restore for the crew.
- An extra life — if an operator dies once in the next three combats, they revive instantly.

### the tithe

Something bad. Always structured as a **long-term corruption cost**:

- +10 corruption to every operator immediately.
- All cards of a certain type (ATTACK, SKILL, or POWER) gain a sin tag.
- A random relic in your inventory is **inverted** — it now has the opposite effect.
- One operator's character passive is **suppressed** for three combats.
- The crew must survive the next combat **without defending** — any BLOCK cards they play are silently ignored.

## the trick

The trick is that the tithe looks manageable at the moment of offering. **It almost always is**, in isolation. The problem is that pacts are not offered in isolation — the angels *track* which pacts a crew has accepted, and subsequent pacts are weighted based on that history.

A crew that has accepted three corruption-cost pacts will be offered a fourth pact whose tithe is "your corruption cannot be removed for the rest of the run." That pact will have an excellent offering. The crew will vote on it. The vote will matter.

## the dissenters

When the crew votes and the majority accepts a pact, the **dissenters** still have to live with the tithe. GENESIS has had fistfights about this. GENESIS has had crews split up because of this. The rule is:

> The pact binds the party, not the accepters.

This is why the vote phase matters. This is why every GENESIS operator learns to read the pact text carefully and to count votes faster than they can read. A dissenter who could not rally a majority has three options: accept the consequence, leave the crew, or make sure the next pact goes their way.

## tithes

A **tithe** is a different mechanism. Unlike a pact, which is a single one-time transaction, a tithe is a **recurring cost** — the crew agrees to pay the angel something every combat, in exchange for an ongoing benefit.

Tithes are offered less often than pacts but are much more powerful. Common tithes:

- **Pay 5 HP per combat, all operators** → gain +1 card draw per turn, all operators, for the rest of the run.
- **Lose 1 gold per turn in combat** → every non-attack card also applies Weak to a random enemy.
- **Sacrifice one gem from inventory per combat** → gems in sockets are treated as tier+1.

Tithes persist across every combat until the run ends or the crew explicitly breaks the tithe at a shrine (which costs a full relic).

## how to play them

The right answer depends on how deep you plan to go. In a short run targeting the Outer Nexus only, accept generous pacts freely — the tithe won't compound enough to matter. In a deep run targeting the Void Core, reject almost every pact and almost every tithe, because Metatron *reads* the history of acceptance as a measure of the crew's corruption fluency, and a crew that has accepted many pacts will find the final room much harder.

**The fourth outcome of the Void Core we do not name — most crews who reach it got there by accepting too many pacts.**
