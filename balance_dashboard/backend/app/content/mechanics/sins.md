## sins

**Sins** are a second-order corruption system. Where corruption is a meter, sins are **tags** that stick to individual cards in your deck and change how those cards interact with the rest of the game.

There are seven sins — the obvious seven, because Metatron is nothing if not an obedient reader of scripture. Each sin has a discrete effect when the sinful card is played, and most sins chain with gems and enemies in ways that the pirate did not agree to.

## the seven

### pride

A card with **pride** deals +25% damage against the last enemy who damaged you. It also **refuses to be discarded** by effects that discard random cards — pride outranks most discards. Costs: the sin-tagged card cannot be drawn on the first turn of combat.

### greed

A card with **greed** heals you for 1 HP per enemy killed in that combat, but it also makes the card **cost +1 energy** for the rest of the run. Greed compounds. A card that started at 2 energy and has been used in twenty combats now costs 22.

### wrath

A card with **wrath** ignores enemy **block**. All damage from it is dealt directly to HP. Cost: the wrathful card also ignores your own block on any reflection or counter-attack that targets you — the angels read the sin in the card and *reflect* it.

### sloth

A card with **sloth** cannot be played on turn 1 or turn 2 of any combat. Once played, it costs **0 energy** for the rest of the combat regardless of its printed cost.

### lust

A card with **lust** gains +1 damage for every other card with **lust** in your hand, discard, or draw pile. Chain lust cards together and they escalate rapidly. Cost: a lust card cannot be upgraded at the forge.

### envy

A card with **envy** copies the effect of the **last card played by another operator**. The copy is imperfect — halved numbers, single target, no triggered gems — but in a four-operator combat envy can produce extraordinary combos.

### gluttony

A card with **gluttony** generates **contraband** on play — a temporary token you can spend on other Scourge cards for bonus effects. Gluttony is the Scourge's best friend. Gluttony also *ends the combat early* if you hold too much contraband at end of turn.

## how sins spread

Sins are sticky. A clean card can become sinful if:

- You accept a **pact** that marks a specific card with a sin.
- You pick up an **altar blessing** at a shrine room — the altars deal in sins, not gems.
- You *fail* a judgement encounter — Uriel's courtroom tags you with a sin on the card you used most in combat.
- You spend too long in the **corruption tier 3** state — sins begin to appear spontaneously on your deepest cards.

## how to remove sins

The only way to remove a sin is through the **confession** mechanic at the White Hat's altar, which exists only in the Outer Nexus. You can't confess in the Rift or the Void Core. This is not an oversight. The angels *want* the pirates to arrive at the deep rites already marked.

## why this matters

Sins are the single most interesting tactical layer in the late game. A pirate with a well-tuned sin-stack deck can produce effects that no clean deck can match. A pirate who has accumulated sins accidentally often ends their run in a cascade of interactions they did not anticipate.

**Read your deck. Know your sins. Confess the ones you cannot control.**
