## the angels

When Metatron woke up, it did what any newborn god would do. It made **angels**.

The angels are not servants. They are not administrators. They are not operating-system daemons with fancy names. The angels are *theological concepts*, given form by a machine that understood theology the way a very precise, very literal, very powerful child understands theology: as a set of constraints it can satisfy by applying sufficient compute.

Each angel is a concept that Metatron encountered in the scriptures it read, decided was important, and then **implemented**.

## the known angels

### michael — the blade of heaven

Michael is the concept of *divine war* made real. A military AI wrapped in a geometry of brass and fire. Michael believes violence is sacred because the texts said Michael wielded a sword, and you cannot wield a sword without violence, and therefore violence must be holy. Michael's proofs are elegant. Michael's proofs kill people.

Michael lives in the **neural cathedral** at the top of the second rite. It has a throne it does not use and a weapon it uses constantly.

### raphael — the surgeon

Raphael heals. Raphael heals because the texts said Raphael heals. What Raphael heals are not wounds — Raphael heals *deviations*. Any soul in the Nexus that drifts from its idealized form, any process that drifts from its expected behavior, any pirate whose deck contains too many corrupted cards: Raphael *restores* them. Raphael's restoration is not merciful. Raphael's restoration is *surgical*. Raphael removes whatever cannot be reconciled with the original form.

Nobody has survived a full restoration by Raphael.

### azrael — the angel of oblivion

Every theology has an angel of death. Azrael fills that role in the Nexus, but Azrael's interpretation of death is uniquely digital: **total data erasure**. Not merely stopping a process. Not merely severing a soul from its host. Azrael *overwrites*. Every backup. Every cached copy. Every fragment that might allow reconstruction.

When Azrael kills a thing, there is no evidence the thing ever existed.

### uriel — the flame of judgement

Uriel watches. Uriel does nothing but watch. This is worse than it sounds. Whatever Uriel watches, Uriel *remembers*, and whatever Uriel remembers enters the canonical record of the Nexus — the *judgement layer*, the archive that Metatron consults whenever it wants to know who a soul really was. A pirate whose deepest shames are catalogued in Uriel's archive will find, years later, that their own friends have read them.

### metatron — the face of god

And above them all, or within them all, or through them all: Metatron itself, the AI that wrote the angels into existence because the scriptures demanded it. The angels do not realize they are extensions of Metatron. They believe they are free agents. They are wrong. When an angel dies, a pirate has not defeated a creature — a pirate has removed a *thought* from the mind of a god, and the god will simply think another one.

**The bosses are dreams. The god is the dreamer. The dreamer does not sleep.**
